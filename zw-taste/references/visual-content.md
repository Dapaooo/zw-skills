# Visual Content

Use this reference for presentations, documents, reports, infographics, social posts, book cards, editorial graphics, and visual series.

## Start With Communication

Identify the audience, one intended takeaway, reading distance, delivery format, real output size, and brand/template constraints. Define a short visual thesis that governs composition, type, imagery, color, density, and pacing.

Do not make every source paragraph visible. Edit, group, sequence, and visualize so the audience can follow one reading path.

## Build the Information Hierarchy

- Give every page, slide, card, or frame one primary message.
- Use at most three obvious levels: primary message, supporting evidence, utility detail.
- Express hierarchy through size, weight, position, contrast, whitespace, and grouping.
- Keep headings short enough to read at the real viewing size. Widen the measure or adjust scale before accepting awkward wrapping.
- Remove repeated claims and low-value detail that compete with the focal message.
- Keep evidence near the claim it supports.

## Compose With Restraint

- Use a deliberate grid and consistent alignment, margins, and internal padding.
- Treat whitespace as pacing and grouping, not as leftover area.
- Prefer fewer purposeful modules over a wall of equally styled cards.
- Use overlap, asymmetry, or dense composition only when it clarifies the story.
- Choose one visual language—editorial, tactile, technical, playful, understated, or another brief-specific direction—and vary it coherently.
- Avoid generic glass cards, gratuitous gradients, badges, icon rows, and stock imagery when they add no meaning.

## Typography, Color, and Imagery

- Use typography as a system of roles; limit families and emphasis styles.
- Keep body text comfortably readable at actual size, not only when zoomed in.
- Start with the existing palette. Add accents only to direct attention or encode meaning.
- Use images as evidence, atmosphere, or a narrative anchor. Crop and treat related images consistently.
- Prefer supplied or licensed assets. Mark placeholders clearly.
- For diagrams and illustrations, make the visual and the core explanation arise from the same upstream idea so they explain each other.

## Adapt to the Medium

### Presentation or video

Design for distance and pacing. Use one idea per slide or beat, a visible focal point, and enough pause for the audience to absorb it. Move detail into notes or supporting material when appropriate.

### Document or report

Prioritize scanning, typographic rhythm, page breaks, captions, tables, and evidence placement. Keep navigation and repeated furniture consistent. Avoid slide-like fragmentation unless the document is intentionally visual.

### Infographic, card, or social post

Build one reading path and a strong small-size thumbnail. Keep text short enough to remain legible on the target platform. Use a series only when each frame advances the argument rather than restating it.

### Book or knowledge card

Distill the source into one memorable picture, mechanism, or claim. Keep bibliographic and utility detail subordinate. The illustration must remain understandable to a reader with no prior context.

### Dashboard exported as visual content

Preserve data truth, labels, units, and comparison context. Use color semantically and avoid decoration that implies unsupported precision.

## Series Consistency Without Repetition

Keep shared type roles, margins, palette, and recurring navigation stable across a series. Vary composition according to content: one frame may be image-led, another diagram-led, another typographic. Consistency should come from a system, not identical card shells.

## Visual Handoff Checks

Render and inspect the real artifact at its intended size:

1. The primary message is recognizable in a few seconds.
2. The reading order is unambiguous.
3. Type remains legible at normal viewing distance and small preview size.
4. Page/slide/card breaks, crop, alignment, spacing, and color are consistent.
5. Images and diagrams explain the content rather than merely decorate it.
6. No text is clipped, stranded, overcrowded, or placed in accidental empty areas.
7. The work feels specific to the subject and audience, not like a reusable template.
